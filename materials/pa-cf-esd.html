Five examples and why the material fits

Each example connects a part’s job to the material properties that can help. Application ideas are reasoned suggestions from the linked data. Documented prints link to a creator’s build; the explanation of material choice is our analysis.

01Application idea

PCB assembly locating nest

Support a circuit board by its edges during manual component assembly.

Why this material
The dedicated dissipative nylon grade offers a path for controlled static dissipation while its stiffness keeps the board supports from moving under light assembly forces.
What it provides
The dissipative fixture can support board handling while participating in a verified static-control arrangement.
Tradeoff
Measure resistance on the finished print and its grounding arrangement; the material designation alone does not qualify the workstation.

02Application idea

Component transfer tray

Carry unpowered electronic components between adjacent assembly stations in shaped pockets.

Why this material
A static-dissipative compound is more appropriate to evaluate for charge control than ordinary insulating carbon-filled nylon, while rigid walls keep pockets separated and repeatable.
What it provides
The dissipative pocket walls can avoid the highly insulating surfaces of ordinary nylon component trays.
Tradeoff
Thin dividers can snap because this grade has low elongation; assess print orientation and actual resistance.

03Application idea

Test-fixture probe support plate

Position a low-force probe assembly around a board under test.

Why this material
The grade combines dissipative behavior with stiffness, so the support plate can hold probe locations while participating in a deliberately designed static-control arrangement.
What it provides
The rigid support can maintain probe spacing while contributing to the fixture's verified static-control path.
Tradeoff
Its weak Z direction demands careful boss orientation; dissipative plastic must not be assumed electrically insulating around live conductors.

04Application idea

Electronic-part gripper jaw shells

Fit replaceable rigid jaw shells to a gripper handling unpowered housings or boards.

Why this material
Dissipative nylon can reduce charge accumulation relative to an insulating jaw, and a stiff shell maintains the selected contact geometry under modest handling loads.
What it provides
The stiff dissipative shells can preserve jaw shape while reducing charge accumulation relative to insulating contacts.
Tradeoff
The brittle grade needs generous radii, and contact force and resistance must be verified for the actual part.

05Application idea

Warm electronics-process fixture spacer

Space a board holder above a mildly heated bench process fixture.

Why this material
The catalog combines dissipative behavior with published loaded heat-deflection data, providing a starting point for evaluating a spacer exposed to modest heat during electronics work.
What it provides
The spacer can combine board clearance, moderate heat tolerance and a verified dissipative contact path.
Tradeoff
Heat-deflection temperature is not a service rating, and surface resistance must be checked after processing and conditioning.

How to read these values

Values describe representative grades, not every spool. Manufacturer datasheets and independent tests retain their source labels and dates. Printed orientation, test method, conditioning and formulation can change results. Unknown values are —. A listed use is not a certification. Shore A, Shore D and Rockwell hardness are separate scales.
