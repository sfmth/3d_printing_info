Read the full material overview

Glass-fibre polypropylene: filament tensile yield of 46.6 MPa, more than double the carbon-fibre grade, at the cost of density rising to 1.12 g/cm3 and moisture uptake to 0.11 %. Priced from $46/kg, it is the cheapest fibre-reinforced engineering filament here.

Applications

PP-GF turns polypropylene into a firmer material for chemically resistant fixtures and equipment rather than flexible hinges. The PP matrix retains low water uptake, while the glass reinforcement supplies rigidity and helps printed parts retain their shape. Prusa demonstrates pipette stands and vial racks with Prusament PP Glass Fiber, making laboratory positioning hardware a concrete example of the material's role.

The benefit is stable support around compatible chemicals without the moisture conditioning changes associated with nylon. The costs include abrasive filament, a rough surface and poorer reproduction of tiny detail. Filled PP should not inherit unfilled PP's hinge behavior, and chemical resistance is not universal: check concentration and temperature for the actual fluid. The manufacturer also shows a printed exhaust-manifold shape solely as a visual example, which must not be mistaken for a service-temperature claim.

How to read these values

Values describe representative grades, not every spool. Manufacturer datasheets and independent tests retain their source labels and dates. Printed orientation, test method, conditioning and formulation can change results. Unknown values are —. A listed use is not a certification. Shore A, Shore D and Rockwell hardness are separate scales.
