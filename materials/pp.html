Read the full material overview

The lightest common filament at 0.91 g/cm³ and effectively non-hygroscopic at 0.04 % uptake, with unbeatable living-hinge fatigue life — but it bonds to almost no bed surface and warps badly.

Applications

Unfilled polypropylene is used for flexible clips, living hinges, packaging prototypes and chemically resistant equipment. Its distinguishing combination is ductility, repeated bending capability and very low water uptake. A thin hinge can bend while thicker walls provide the body of a container; a mounting clip can yield during assembly without requiring the brittle response of a fibre-filled grade. The documented stereo brackets below explicitly used PP for flexibility.

That flexibility is a disadvantage when a part must hold a precise position or carry a sustained load: PP has limited stiffness and can creep. It also adheres poorly to many printing surfaces and can shrink or warp substantially, so fit and hinge quality depend on the process. The familiar molded-PP living hinge is useful application context, but its lifetime cannot simply be assigned to a layered print.

How to read these values

Values describe representative grades, not every spool. Manufacturer datasheets and independent tests retain their source labels and dates. Printed orientation, test method, conditioning and formulation can change results. Unknown values are —. A listed use is not a certification. Shore A, Shore D and Rockwell hardness are separate scales.
