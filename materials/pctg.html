Read the full material overview

A copolyester with more cyclohexanedimethanol than PETG: notched Izod on moulded specimens reaches 93–94 kJ/m² and elongation at break exceeds 200 %, though printed layer adhesion measures below PETG.

Applications

PCTG is useful where impact tolerance and ductility matter more than maximum rigidity. COEX lists mechanical parts, jigs, protective casings and covers for its PCTG Prime grade. Clear grades also make the polymer attractive for viewing covers, although the black grade on that source page should not be presented as a transparent example.

PCTG’s impressive molded-specimen impact and elongation results do not directly describe every printed part. Layer bonding can be the limiting feature, and published printed-part creep testing makes it a poor default for a continuously loaded precision fixture. Its niche is tough, readily printed functional parts and covers, with actual grade, color and print orientation checked against the application.

  • Protective casings and covers ↗

    FDM · COEX PCTG Prime; source product is black · Manufacturer-listed applications

    COEX lists casings and covers for PCTG Prime, connecting its toughness and chemical resistance to functional use. This is a manufacturer application recommendation, not a certified impact-protection case study.

    These are explicit COEX applications. Toughness is valuable where a fixture is handled or knocked, while creep and printed layer strength remain relevant to accurate workholding.

How to read these values

Values describe representative grades, not every spool. Manufacturer datasheets and independent tests retain their source labels and dates. Printed orientation, test method, conditioning and formulation can change results. Unknown values are —. A listed use is not a certification. Shore A, Shore D and Rockwell hardness are separate scales.
