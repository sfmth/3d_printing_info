PVDF

A fluoropolymer with outstanding chemical and UV resistance at 1.71 g/cm³ and $251/kg. The only material in the dataset with a genuinely severe fume hazard: above 315 °C it releases hydrofluoric acid and hydrogen cyanide, and local exhaust is mandatory.

Health & Safety

PVDF — Health & Safety
Property and unitPublished value and conditionsSources and dates
Fume toxicity (1–5)Very high — toxic decomposition products; local exhaust mandatory. Above 315 °C thermal decomposition yields hydrofluoric acid, hydrogen cyanide and carbon monoxide
  • FluorX PVDF Safety Data Sheet v1.1 · 2026-08 · msds
Hazardous species emittedAbove 315 °C: hydrofluoric acid, hydrogen cyanide, phenols, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide and dense smoke. HF is extremely corrosive, its skin burns may not be immediately visible or painful, and exposure can be fatal by skin absorption, inhalation or ingestion
  • FluorX PVDF Safety Data Sheet v1.1 · 2026-08 · msds
Ventilation / filtration requirement (1–5)Local exhaust to outside / fume hood — the SDS states local exhaust is preferred
  • FluorX PVDF Safety Data Sheet v1.1 · 2026-08 · msds
Dust & fibre handling hazardMolten polymer causes thermal burns and must not be pulled off the skin. If HF is detected during a decomposition event, cut the heat but keep the extraction running, ventilate, and clear non-essential people from the area
  • FluorX PVDF Safety Data Sheet v1.1 · 2026-08 · msds
