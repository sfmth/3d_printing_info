Read the full material overview

Semi-crystalline PET with carbon fibre — a genuinely different material from PETG-CF, reaching 182–205 °C heat deflection but needing an 80 °C dry, a heated chamber and speeds halved to 100 mm/s.

Choose a material for a purpose

Five examples and why the material fits

Each example connects a part’s job to the material properties that can help. Application ideas are reasoned suggestions from the linked data. Documented prints link to a creator’s build; the explanation of material choice is our analysis.

01Documented print

Apache-case speaker grille and trim

A fitted grille surrounds and covers speakers installed in a portable equipment case.

Why this material
The existing creator example verifies PET-CF rather than PETG-CF. Its stiff PET composite matrix is relevant to a thin grille that should retain its shape, although that rationale is inferred rather than creator testimony.
What it provides
High printed stiffness helps slender grille features retain their intended shape during ordinary case handling.
Tradeoff
PET-CF is brittle, so grille ribs and mounting tabs need generous geometry.

02Application idea

Warm-process positioning nest

A shaped nest locates a part during a controlled, mildly heated workshop process.

Why this material
PET-CF combines a high modulus with substantially higher reported heat deflection than PETG-CF. That makes it a candidate when a rigid locator must work near warmth that would soften simpler polyesters.
What it provides
Higher thermal capability expands the range of warm locating tasks worth evaluating beyond ordinary PETG.
Tradeoff
Grade, annealing condition and printed orientation govern usable performance; an HDT value is not a continuous service rating.

03Application idea

Printer enclosure camera arm

A short arm positions a monitoring camera inside a warm printer enclosure.

Why this material
PET-CF offers stiffness and heat resistance for a bracket exposed to enclosure warmth. Its PET matrix is distinct from PETG, so the choice should be tied to the actual PET-CF grade.
What it provides
Thermal capability and stiffness help the camera position remain useful inside a warm enclosure.
Tradeoff
Weakness across layers calls for an orientation that keeps bending loads within the print plane.

04Application idea

Motor-test sensor stand

A stand positions a noncontact sensor beside a warm benchtop motor experiment.

Why this material
PET-CF is useful when sensor alignment needs stiffness and the nearby equipment generates heat. Its high printed modulus supports a compact fixture without claiming the fatigue performance of a metal stand.
What it provides
A stiff stand reduces immediate sensor displacement under modest adjustment and cable-routing forces.
Tradeoff
Brittleness makes this a positioning accessory, not a guard or motor-retention component.

05Application idea

Oily-workbench inspection fixture

A contoured cradle supports a small oily component during dimensional inspection.

Why this material
The PET matrix has useful resistance to oils and common chemicals, while carbon fibre supplies rigidity. This combination suits a fixture that must maintain its contour during brief handling and inspection.
What it provides
Oil resistance helps maintain a usable contact surface while the rigid cradle supports consistent positioning.
Tradeoff
Validate actual cleaners and temperatures; catalog chemical resistance is not universal solvent compatibility.

How to read these values

Values describe representative grades, not every spool. Manufacturer datasheets and independent tests retain their source labels and dates. Printed orientation, test method, conditioning and formulation can change results. Unknown values are —. A listed use is not a certification. Shore A, Shore D and Rockwell hardness are separate scales.
