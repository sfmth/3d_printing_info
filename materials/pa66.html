Read the full material overview

Higher-melting than PA6 and common as an injection-moulding resin, but effectively absent from the filament market — no unfilled PA66 filament was found in any catalogue searched, so this entry is deliberately sparse.

Each example connects a part’s job to the material properties that can help. Application ideas are reasoned suggestions from the linked data. Documented prints link to a creator’s build; the explanation of material choice is our analysis.

01Application idea

Conditional idea: PA66 guide bushing trial

Evaluate a printed PA66 bushing only if a documented, printable unfilled grade becomes available.

Why this material
Extruded PA66 stock has published sliding and wear properties, which motivates a sliding-contact experiment; these stock data do not establish the performance of a printed bushing.
What it provides
If validated, stock-like wear behavior could make a replaceable printed guide useful; no printed advantage is established.
Tradeoff
The catalog found no unfilled PA66 filament, so availability, printing conditions, conditioning and wear must all be established first.
Supporting properties and sources
Market availability (1–5)
Special order, 1–2 makers worldwide — no unfilled PA66 filament appeared in any catalogue searched, and the 3DXTech nylon range offers PA6, PA12 and a 6/66 copolymer but no straight PA66
Wear resistance & friction
Wear factor 200e-10 in3*min/(ft*lb*h) on extruded PA66 stock (ASTM D3702); Ensinger lists very good slide and wear properties
Moisture absorption (hygroscopicity) (1–5)
Very high (>2 %)

02Application idea

Conditional idea: PA66 gear development coupon

Use a small gear as a development specimen after sourcing a verified unfilled PA66 filament.

Why this material
PA66 stock wear data provide a reason to investigate tooth contact, but printed anisotropy and humidity response remain unknown for an unavailable filament grade.
What it provides
If testing confirms useful wear behavior, a printed gear could serve a replaceable development mechanism.
Tradeoff
Do not transfer stock tensile or thermal figures into gear ratings; qualify the filament, process and conditioned print before any working application.

04Application idea

Conditional idea: warm fixture spacer trial

Investigate a printed spacer in a controlled warm fixture after verifying a printable PA66 grade.

Why this material
Extruded PA66 stock has load-dependent heat-deflection results, suggesting thermal testing is relevant while also showing why melting point alone cannot define usable temperature.
What it provides
If validated under load, thermal stability could help a printed spacer preserve its fixture clearance.
Tradeoff
The catalog has no printed PA66 thermal data or verified filament, so stock results cannot justify a working load or temperature.
Supporting properties and sources

05Application idea

Conditional idea: hand-operated positioning lever trial

Evaluate a printed PA66 lever on a low-load development fixture only after documenting the filament.

Why this material
Stock tensile and elongation data motivate investigating a tough handling part, but processing and layer bonding can change the response completely.
What it provides
If verified experimentally, suitable printed toughness could permit a lever that tolerates routine handling.
Tradeoff
No unfilled PA66 filament was verified, and stock properties plus CoPA conditioning examples must not be presented as printed PA66 measurements.

How to read these values

Values describe representative grades, not every spool. Manufacturer datasheets and independent tests retain their source labels and dates. Printed orientation, test method, conditioning and formulation can change results. Unknown values are —. A listed use is not a certification. Shore A, Shore D and Rockwell hardness are separate scales.
