Read the full material overview

The entry point to the sulfones at $250/kg: Tg 185-187 °C against PPSU's 230 °C, but 8 % printed elongation against 3 %, so it is markedly less brittle. Only 3DXTech sells it as filament in this survey.

Choose a material for a purpose

Five examples and why the material fits

Each example connects a part’s job to the material properties that can help. Application ideas are reasoned suggestions from the linked data. Documented prints link to a creator’s build; the explanation of material choice is our analysis.

01Application idea

Warm instrument housing prototype

Print a housing to evaluate a low-voltage laboratory instrument around moderate heat.

Why this material
PSU is identified for high-temperature housings and has published printed heat-deflection data, so it is a candidate when ordinary plastics lose shape during a controlled trial.
What it provides
Heat-capable rigidity can help a validated housing keep connectors and internal components positioned during a warm trial.
Tradeoff
Its thermal capability is below PPSU, and the printed housing needs direct load and temperature validation.

02Application idea

Hot-water experiment hose bracket

Print a bracket that positions flexible tubing in a benchtop hot-water experiment.

Why this material
PSU's documented hot-water-fitting application makes it relevant to evaluate for external tube positioning without relying on the print to contain pressure.
What it provides
A verified PSU bracket can maintain tube routing through a warm experiment without serving as a pressure boundary.
Tradeoff
Do not transfer bulk service figures to the print, and test cleaning chemicals because ketones and aromatic solvents can craze PSU.
Supporting properties and sources

03Application idea

Warm low-voltage connector standoff

Print a standoff for a noncritical connector evaluation fixture.

Why this material
PSU combines an insulating material classification with rigidity and printed heat-deflection data, giving a reason to consider it when a shaped spacer must separate warm components.
What it provides
An insulating rigid spacer can maintain connector separation while tolerating the validated fixture's heat exposure.
Tradeoff
Surface-resistance data does not certify printed dielectric performance, and PSU is not a substitute for PPSU in repeated steam cycles.

04Application idea

Temperature-test sample tray

Print a tray that keeps small nonclinical samples separated during thermal experiments.

Why this material
The catalog identifies PSU for trays and gives printed mechanical and thermal data, making it a candidate when room-temperature organizers are too soft for a controlled trial.
What it provides
Heat-capable stiffness can keep the tested sample compartments separated and level through a thermal trial.
Tradeoff
A coupon HDT is not a working-temperature specification, and the tray must be tested under its actual sample load.

05Application idea

Solvent-free warm assembly fixture

Print a fixture that holds parts during a dry warm assembly step.

Why this material
PSU's rigidity and published printed heat-deflection data make it relevant where a fixture sees heat but does not need PPSU's repeated-steam capability.
What it provides
Thermal rigidity can help a verified fixture preserve assembly alignment throughout the warm process step.
Tradeoff
Ketones, esters and aromatic cleaners can attack PSU, so the assembly and cleaning workflow must be compatible.

How to read these values

Values describe representative grades, not every spool. Manufacturer datasheets and independent tests retain their source labels and dates. Printed orientation, test method, conditioning and formulation can change results. Unknown values are —. A listed use is not a certification. Shore A, Shore D and Rockwell hardness are separate scales.
