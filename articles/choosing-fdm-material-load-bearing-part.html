Choosing an FDM material for a load-bearing part

Screen specific PLA, PETG, ASA and PC Blend grades by the failure mode, then check orientation, sustained loading and the limits of published coupon tests.

Editorial synthesis assisted by AI, based on the linked supplier documents and experimental research. No mechanical testing was performed for this article.

Choose the failure criterion before choosing the polymer

Engineering interpretation. For a bracket, fixture or support, begin with the permitted deflection, the load direction and how long the force acts. A grade that limits immediate bending may still deform during a long hold, or fail where the part pulls across layers. PLA, PETG, ASA and PC Blend are candidates to screen against those requirements; the evidence here does not establish a universal winner. ASTM International [details] · Stratasys [details]

The table reports specific Prusament printed-coupon results for tensile modulus and yield strength. It is useful for deciding what to investigate next. Its missing test conditions and lack of build-Z tensile results prevent it from supplying design allowables for an arbitrary printed part. Prusa Polymers a.s. [details] · Prusa Polymers a.s. [details]

Turn “strong enough” into a requirement

Separate initial elastic deflection, yielding, ultimate tensile failure and rupture. Modulus describes the initial stress–strain response; yield is a different point on that response. Neither quantity measures the force needed to break your complete bracket. Its section, holes, fasteners and load application determine how a nominal force becomes a local stress. ASTM International [details]

Engineering interpretation. Write down the force and its lever arm, the allowed displacement at the working surface, the load duration, and the temperature and moisture range. Also identify drops, repeated cycles and chemical contact. These become distinct acceptance checks. A single maximum-strength entry cannot answer all of them. ASTM International [details] · ASTM International [details]

Stiffness and tensile strength answer different questions

An independent experiment by Amza and colleagues illustrates the distinction: its unaged Fillamentum PLA Extrafill specimens had a higher tensile modulus than its PrimaSelect PETG specimens, while the PETG specimens had the higher ultimate tensile strength. These were ASTM D638 tests on Ender-3 prints, rather than the supplier ISO yield tests above. Their absolute results are kept out of this table. Amza et al. / Polymers [details]

Engineering interpretation. For a locating fixture, a deflection limit may govern before yielding. For a part that must carry a short overload, a yield criterion may govern instead. Investigate the relevant grade property and the part’s section together. Do not replace yield with ultimate or rupture stress, and do not divide an external force by a convenient nominal area without checking the actual load path. ASTM International [details] · Stratasys [details]

An on-edge coupon is not a build-Z tensile test

In these Prusament drawings, “Horizontal” is a flat coupon in XY. “Vertical xz” is an on-edge coupon whose long axis still follows X. It does not mean the tensile force pulls along the machine’s build Z. The two columns therefore cannot establish interlayer tensile strength or a cross-layer reduction factor. Prusa Polymers a.s. [details]

Engineering interpretation. Trace force through the proposed part and choose a build orientation around that path. A layer interface crossing a heavily loaded root needs evidence relevant to that interface. If a candidate sheet supplies only in-plane coupons, request directional data or test representative specimens and the component. A polymer name cannot resolve that gap. Stratasys [details] · Prusa Polymers a.s. [details]

A brief tensile test does not settle a long hold or an impact

Fischbach and Weinberg observed continuing flexural creep in printed BASF Ultrafuse PLA during a long room-temperature hold below its glass transition. The long-duration result came from one specimen, so it supports the need to investigate time-dependent deformation, rather than a general life prediction or a universal strength reduction. Fischbach and Weinberg [details]

Impact testing introduces another question. ASTM D256 identifies specimen preparation, notch, conditioning and failure mode as factors affecting the result. A notched Izod result, an unnotched Charpy result and a coupon that did not break are not interchangeable numbers. No impact ranking is inferred from the tensile table. ASTM International [details]

Engineering interpretation. For a continuously loaded support, measure displacement during a representative hold at the service temperature. For a bolted fixture, assess the assembled joint through the required hold and operating cycles. For a protective component, reproduce the relevant impact and inspect its mounting points. Specify acceptance limits before testing. ASTM International [details] · Fischbach and Weinberg [details] · ASTM International [details]

Use industrial examples as context for the decision

Stratasys describes Volvo Trucks using industrial FDM for lifting devices and ancillary tooling. The cited account does not assign an exact grade or load rating to each component. It establishes a tooling application context; it cannot qualify a desktop-printed PLA, ASA or PC Blend substitute. Stratasys [details]

Engineering interpretation. Expand the candidate list when these four grades lack the necessary directional, thermal, environmental or long-term evidence. A conditioned nylon, reinforced grade or higher-temperature polymer may deserve testing, but each brings a new grade and process to qualify. This pilot supplies no common-condition results that rank those alternatives. Start that comparison with the missing measurement, rather than assuming the family name solves the problem. ASTM International [details]

Before releasing a part

The following is an engineering verification sequence for the stated use. Set the required margin and acceptance limits through the project’s design process; the reported supplier values and unspecified ± terms do not define them. ASTM International [details]

  1. Record the exact grade, color or lot where relevant, filament preparation, machine, build orientation, toolpath and post-processing. Keep the tested process with the result.

  2. Measure initial deflection and permanent set using the real load path, supports, fasteners and critical dimensions. Include the orientations that control the proposed failure mode.

  3. Run the needed sustained-load, cycle, temperature, moisture and impact checks with explicit pass/fail limits. A short tensile coupon result cannot substitute for these conditions. ASTM International [details]

  4. If the evidence cannot support the required margin, change the geometry, material or manufacturing route and repeat the affected checks. Preserve the failed result and the reason for the change.

tensile test scope
Tensile properties depend on specimen preparation, rate and environment; modulus characterizes the initial response. Testing over different load durations and environments is needed for engineering use. ASTM D638 and ISO 527-1 have differing technical content.
Public Significance and Use §§4.1, 4.3–4.5 and Note 5; Scope §1.1 and Note 1. ASTM International [details]
stiffness vs strength
In the unaged control groups, the tested PLA specimens had a higher modulus while the tested PETG specimens had a higher ultimate tensile strength. This is an independent printed-grade result, not a yield-stress comparison or a statement about all PLA and PETG.
§2 and Table 1 (materials and printing); §3.2, Table 2, PLA Control and PETG Control columns. Amza et al. / Polymers [details]

creep below tg
One printed Ultrafuse PLA specimen continued to creep under the long room-temperature bending hold below its glass transition. This evidence calls for a sustained-load check but does not supply a general lifetime prediction.
§3.1/Table 1 (material and process), §3.3 (test protocol), §4.2/Figure 11 and §5 (long-duration observations). Fischbach and Weinberg [details]
impact test scope
Specimen preparation, notch, conditioning and failure type affect impact results. A no-break observation is not an unlimited impact resistance. Results from different impact methods and specimen configurations do not establish a common ranking.
Public Scope §1.1 and Notes 3–4; Significance and Use §§5.2, 5.7–5.9. ASTM International [details]
