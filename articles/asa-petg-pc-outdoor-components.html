ASA vs PETG vs PC for outdoor components

Separate UV, moisture, sun heating and appearance requirements using named FDM grades, documented exposure protocols and specific verification tests.

3D Printing Info · Published 2026-10-18 · Revised 2026-10-18

Editorial synthesis assisted by AI, based on the linked supplier documents and experimental research. No mechanical testing was performed for this article.

The selection decisionGrade measurementsTest conditionsBefore releasing a partSources

Choose an exposed system, then verify the hot, wet part

Engineering interpretation. A documented ASA grade is a reasonable candidate to investigate for an exposed housing or bracket. In Stratasys’s common weathering program, its natural ASA showed a smaller change in break stress than the tested PC. This supports a decision about those printed grades and that exposure; it does not qualify every ASA spool or rule out a separately stabilized PC system. Stratasys [details] · Stratasys [details] · Stratasys [details]

Engineering interpretation. Keep PETG in the candidate list when its exact grade and color meet the use case, but require weathering evidence. A transparent PrimaSelect PETG experiment used a different UV-B protocol and cannot be inserted into the ASA/PC comparison. For a sun-heated loaded part, the unresolved decision is retained component performance while hot and wet, rather than the polymer name alone. Amza et al. / Polymers [details] · ASTM International [details]

Keep the exposure protocols and specimen roles separate

The first group shares a supplier, printer and stated QUV exposure. The PETG group remains separate, and color differences belong to their own flat plaques. Missing lamp, orientation or statistical information stays visible rather than being supplied from another experiment. Stratasys [details] · Stratasys [details] · Amza et al. / Polymers [details]

Unchanged stiffness can hide lost strength

The exposed PC cohort has a higher reported tensile modulus and a lower break stress than its control. A stiffness-only screen would miss that loss of resistance to rupture. The natural ASA result changes less in break stress in the same program, but the source provides no UV-table uncertainty with which to judge small differences. Stratasys [details] · Stratasys [details] · Stratasys [details] · Stratasys [details]

Engineering interpretation. These exposures combine light, heating and moisture. They show the net response of a printed specimen after a defined treatment; they do not isolate the contribution of each mechanism or measure years of loaded service. Recheck the load path, critical build direction and fastened joint after aging instead of treating a retained modulus as a complete durability result. Stratasys [details] · ASTM International [details]

Specify appearance independently from strength

The two reported ASA plaque color differences are 9.23 and 0.86; the corresponding PC results are 33.48 and 32.7. Retaining both values makes their spread visible. They are individual color outputs, not confidence intervals or a universal pass threshold. Do not replace them with a claim that ASA never changes color. Stratasys [details] · Stratasys [details] · Stratasys [details] · Stratasys [details]

Engineering interpretation. The PETG study also observed yellowing and darkening, but did not supply a compatible numerical color difference. A clear sensor window or light cover therefore needs separate transmission, haze and color limits alongside mechanical requirements. A cosmetic enclosure can fail an appearance requirement before a structural failure occurs. Amza et al. / Polymers [details] · Stratasys [details]

A protected PC system is a different candidate

Covestro identifies Makrolon 2407 as UV-stabilized injection-molding resin. That provides a concrete example of grade-specific stabilization; it supplies no outdoor lifetime for an arbitrary PC filament. Its automotive optical portfolio also treats resin, color and protective hard coat as a system, including named Makrolon AG2677 exterior substrates. These are industrial material/application descriptions, not FFF qualifications. Covestro [details] · Covestro [details]

Engineering interpretation. If PC is attractive for the part’s other requirements, obtain weathering results for the actual filament and surface protection. Evaluate coating adhesion, edges, drilled holes and maintenance after printing. A protected molded optical component is useful design context, but its formulation, surface and approvals do not transfer to a printed bracket. Covestro [details] · Stratasys [details]

Make sunlight temperature and wetness part of the load case

Outdoor durability depends on the site’s radiation, wetness, temperature and pollutants; a ranking from one climate need not survive another climate. Define the intended exposure before choosing between the grades. A chamber’s hours are not a calendar-life specification, even when its lamp irradiance is known. ASTM International [details] · ASTM International [details]

Engineering interpretation. For a loaded outdoor housing, measure the actual part temperature in its intended color and mounting arrangement. Test the aged component at that temperature with the expected wet/dry state, sustained load and assembly preload. The room-conditioned tensile results above do not establish resistance to warm deflection, creep, water ingress or thermal cycling. Stratasys [details] · ASTM International [details]

Read the printed specimen and exposure records

Open a record for the exact build direction, printing information, pre-test conditioning and exposure cycle. The ASA and PC UV tables use ten tensile specimens per condition; the PETG means use five and report standard error. Color plaques are a different specimen population, so their ΔE results are not attached to the strength coupons. Stratasys [details] · Stratasys [details] · Amza et al. / Polymers [details]

The tests that resolve an outdoor selection

  1. Engineering interpretation. Specify the commercial grade, color, print direction, thickness, surface finish and any coating. Set separate acceptance limits for load, deflection, fastener retention, sealing and appearance before aging. Covestro [details] · Stratasys [details]

  2. Engineering interpretation. Expose candidate and control specimens together using a documented spectrum, irradiance, temperature and wetness cycle. Add a thermal/wet control if you need to separate the effects of UV from conditioning. Relate the laboratory program to field specimens from the intended climate. ASTM International [details] · ASTM International [details]

  3. Engineering interpretation. After exposure, test the finished assembly under representative warm, wet and sustained loading. Inspect the critical build direction, joints, coating edges and clearances; include cycling or freezing when the intended service contains those conditions. Stratasys [details] · ASTM International [details]

  4. Engineering interpretation. For visible or transmitting parts, measure color and any required transmission/haze after exposure. Retest when pigment, stabilizer package, coating or printing process changes. Public evidence here leaves those grade-specific acceptance and service-life decisions open.
