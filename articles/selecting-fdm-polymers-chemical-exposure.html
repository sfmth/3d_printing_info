Selecting FDM polymers for chemical exposure

Choose candidates by the named liquid, concentration, temperature, exposure time and load; separate immersion observations from stress cracking, permeation and retained properties.

Specify the exposure before choosing the polymer

Engineering interpretation. For a fixture near a cleaning station, begin with the actual cleaner and dilution, its hottest contact temperature, dwell time, cleaning frequency and assembly load. Then seek a test for that exact grade and print route. A family compatibility chart is an initial screen; it cannot supply the retained stiffness, joint force or leakage limit of the part.

The available printed-grade experiments below can reject some candidate/exposure combinations and motivate others for testing. They do not support a common numerical ranking of PP, PVDF, POM, PA, PC, PPS and PEEK. Unloaded immersion also leaves environmental stress cracking unresolved. ISO [details]

Read the test boundary before using the result

Prusa used 100% infill, two perimeters and 0.2 mm layers with material profiles, then tested tensile strength to ISO 527 and Charpy impact to ISO 179. A per-endpoint sample count, scatter, exact revisions and recovery/drying interval are absent. Changes after removal from a bath need not equal properties while immersed. The public graphs are not a tabulated design dataset. Prusa Polymers Team [details]

Apium used DIN EN ISO 527-2:2012-06 type 1BA coupons, 100% infill, three layer-height groups and three specimens per condition. Its public page gives compatibility/appearance observations, without publishing retained tensile endpoints. Unknown orientation and annealing prevent transferring the result to another printed PEEK process. Apium Additive Technologies [details]

Immersion, stress cracking and permeation answer different questions

Immersion asks how exposure changes a coupon. Environmental stress cracking asks what happens with simultaneous stress or imposed deformation. A screw boss, snap fit or clamped housing can therefore need a stressed exposure test even when an unrestrained coupon survives. ISO 175 explicitly separates those scopes. ISO [details]

Permeation concerns transport through the polymer; leakage can also follow print interfaces or connected voids. A dimensionally stable coupon and a retained tensile result do not measure either flow path. Arkema likewise distinguishes chemical compatibility from permeability and recommends validating the actual mixture and operating conditions. Arkema [details]

Engineering interpretation. For a liquid-handling prototype, measure both material change and the assembled wall/seal leakage at the intended temperature and pressure. Treat a coating as part of that tested assembly, including joints and defects; a successful coupon alone leaves those paths unresolved.

How to build a useful candidate list

PP: BCN3D PP GF30 is a concrete filament candidate where its stiffness and printing route suit the geometry. Its supplier page gives application positioning, not a named-cleaner exposure matrix. Request that matrix and test the exact 30% glass-filled grade; do not transfer its behavior to unfilled PP. BCN3D [details]

PVDF: Kynar guidance is useful for screening a defined resin/chemical combination and identifying mixture, cycling and stress questions. The industrial resin data do not identify the fabrication route of a printed filament coupon. Obtain the actual filament formulation and printed-wall retention/permeation evidence before selecting PVDF for containment. Arkema [details]

POM and PPS: TECAFORM AH natural and TECATRON natural show how industrial stock materials can enter the shortlist. The former is a POM-C machining material; the latter is extruded PPS. Their broad supplier resistance descriptions do not establish a concentration, dwell time or retained-property limit for FFF parts. Ensinger [details] · Ensinger [details]

Engineering interpretation. PA and PC: choose an exact nylon chemistry or PC blend and include its initial moisture/annealing state. The PC Blend acetone observation is a useful exclusion signal for that tested material. The nylon moisture article addresses water conditioning, which cannot substitute for an arbitrary solvent or formulated-cleaner test. No PA6-versus-PA12 chemical ranking is established here. Prusa Polymers Team [details]

PEEK: the Apium result is a specific route for screening PEEK 450 Natural in propylene glycol. Unspecified brake, transmission or disinfection fluids on the same page cannot define compatibility with every formulation sold under those names. Ask for the actual fluid composition and retained mechanical test before extending the claim. Apium Additive Technologies [details]

Turn a shortlist into a release decision

  1. Engineering interpretation. Write an exposure specification: product and chemical identities, concentration and basis, mixture, liquid/vapor/splash regime, time, temperature, pressure, cleaning cycles and applied load or fixed strain.

  2. Engineering interpretation. Print controls and exposed coupons with the intended wall thickness, interfaces, orientation, batch, drying and annealing. Record mass and critical dimensions before exposure, while wet when relevant, and after the defined recovery interval. A dry-back result cannot silently replace the wet-service result.

  3. Engineering interpretation. Define acceptance around the function: retained strength/stiffness and impact where needed, crack initiation under assembly strain, seal preload, dimensional fit and permeation/leakage. Compare like methods and keep unexposed controls through the same thermal history.

  4. Engineering interpretation. Repeat the combined chemical, temperature and mechanical exposure on the representative component. Select test duration and inspections from the actual duty; a short immersion observation supplies neither a universal safety factor nor a service-life prediction.
