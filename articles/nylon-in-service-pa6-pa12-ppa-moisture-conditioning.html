Nylon in service: PA6, PA12 and PPA after moisture conditioning

Select exact nylon grades for their service moisture state, with separate unreinforced copolymer, carbon-fiber and PPA evidence; distinguish mass uptake, dimensional change and retained mechanical properties.

3D Printing Info · Published 2026-10-18 · Revised 2026-10-18

Editorial synthesis assisted by AI, based on linked manufacturer documents. No new material or component testing was performed.

Select for the moisture state the part must tolerate

Engineering interpretation. Begin with the clearance, allowed deflection and load that the finished part must retain after its expected exposure. A dry-spool printing procedure and a dry-coupon property do not establish those service limits. Qualify the final grade, print route, annealing and exposure together. Polymaker [details] · ASTM International [details]

The evidence below separates a PA6/66 copolymer with no declared fiber reinforcement, two carbon-fiber nylon grades, and a PPA-CF manufacturer comparison. It shows changes within named formulations and the gaps around PPA. It does not provide a common-condition ranking of PA6, PA12 and PPA families. Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details] · Bambu Lab [details]

Drying the spool and conditioning the part serve different purposes

Polymaker distinguishes moisture control during extrusion from the moisture a completed print later acquires. Its recommended sequence places annealing before moisture conditioning; another heat cycle can change the moisture state again. Record these as separate manufacturing and service steps. Polymaker [details]

Engineering interpretation. The word wet needs a protocol. Humid air, a water soak and a part removed from water are different test states. Report the exposure medium, temperature, humidity where applicable, duration, transfer to the test and measured moisture content. A fixed time alone does not show that an arbitrary component has reached its intended state. Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details]

Read paired results within their preparation group

The first two tables use tensile modulus and supplier tensile strength, whose stress endpoint is unspecified. CoPA uses a different annealing route from the Fiberon grades. The PPA table uses flexure and an incompletely defined wet state; those values cannot be pooled with the tensile results. Undefined ± terms are preserved without a confidence interpretation. Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details] · Bambu Lab [details]

For the Fiberon grades, equal soak temperature and duration produced different reported moisture contents: PA6-CF20 averaged 5.30%, while PA12-CF10 averaged 2.92%. These values describe the immersed specimens; they are not equilibrium values for a room or a forecast of exposure time in service. Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details]

Mass uptake, dimensional change and stiffness are separate measurements

CoPA illustrates why the exposure must stay attached to the number. Its humid-air curve reports 2.82% equilibrium uptake at 23 °C and 70% RH, whereas the immersed mechanical group averaged 6.16%. The curve does not identify specimen preparation or an equilibrium criterion. Neither percentage specifies a linear dimensional change. Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details]

Polymaker also shows before/after axis observations for annealed, fully filled 40 mm cubes. The tables report maxima, and omit the moisture exposure and replication protocol. They illustrate the need to measure each critical dimension, but cannot supply a scale factor for a bearing fit, hole or larger assembly. Polymaker [details]

Engineering interpretation. For a locating fixture, set acceptable hole spacing, bore size and displacement under load, then check them after the intended service exposure. A mass-uptake result can help document the state; it cannot replace either the dimensional or loaded-stiffness check. Polymaker [details] · ASTM International [details]

Engineering interpretation. The unfilled comparison here is a PA6/66 copolymer. Matched service-conditioned tests for exact unfilled PA6 and PA12 grades are not established by these sources; obtain those tests before substituting either for the copolymer or carbon-fiber grades.

PPA needs an exact grade and a defined wet protocol

Bambu identifies its PPA-CF matrix as semi-aromatic nylon. Its separate absorption result is 1.30% at 25 °C and 55% RH. The product page gives paired dry/wet XY flexural values, but does not explicitly connect that environment to the wet mechanical tests. Its relatively close page values are a reason to request the missing protocol, rather than evidence of retention after the Fiberon hot-water treatment. Bambu Lab [details] · Bambu Lab [details]

Unfilled PPA is a separate candidate: Siraya explicitly markets Fibreheart PPA as pure polyphthalamide. Its product page lists dry/saturated properties, while the HTML TDS defines a tensile stress at break under ASTM D638. The documents omit a shared conditioning route and disagree on some values. They cannot establish a matched unfilled-versus-filled PPA comparison here. Siraya Tech [details] · Siraya Tech [details]

Engineering interpretation. For a PPA substitution, obtain the exact matrix and reinforcement declaration, the wet exposure and annealing history, and the mechanical result in the load direction that matters. A supplier name containing PAHT does not establish that the matrix is PPA. Bambu Lab [details] · Siraya Tech [details] · Stratasys [details]

Let the service requirement decide the next test

Use the reported changes to choose what to verify. The CoPA and PA6-CF20 XY pairs lose tensile modulus under their respective water-conditioning routes; PA12-CF10 changes less in its reported pair. Different matrices, fiber fractions and annealing prevent attributing that difference to one feature or extending it to every nylon. Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details]

  1. Engineering interpretation. For a fit or positioning fixture, qualify the critical dimensions and loaded displacement in the expected humidity range. Include any assembly constraint rather than applying a free-cube growth factor. Polymaker [details]

  2. Engineering interpretation. For a continuously loaded bracket or bolted joint, measure creep, permanent set or clamp-force retention after conditioning at the service temperature. Short tensile or flexural results do not establish that duration-dependent behavior. Fischbach and Weinberg [details] · ASTM International [details]

  3. Engineering interpretation. For a water-exposed component, reproduce the relevant immersion or wet/dry cycle. Do not convert the published hot-water soak into a time-equivalent ambient-humidity exposure. Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details]

What the tables leave unresolved

The tensile sources omit test temperature, loading rate, specimen count and the statistical definition of ±. Their XY labels do not report raster angle. The Bambu page omits a mechanical method and wet exposure. No table establishes final-part dimensional tolerance, joint preload retention or lifetime. Expand each record for the reported process and the explicit gaps. Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details] · Polymaker [details] · Bambu Lab [details]
