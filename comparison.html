Model notes

  • Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 Combo — launched 2026-01-26 at $449, currently $379. "Combo" adds the CANVAS 4-channel unit, four spool holders and a tophat lid. Distinct from the original Centauri Carbon (CC1, 320 °C) and from the non-Combo CC2; five Centauri siblings are live simultaneously, and several published spec boxes print bare-CC2 weights and dimensions inside Combo contexts.
  • QIDI Q2 — the bare $499 machine. Not the Q2C ($379, no chamber heater, and QIDI states the heater is not retrofittable) and not the Q2 Combo ($649, adds the QIDI Box).
  • Creality K2 SE — shipped late Dec 2025, marketed "K2 SE Open-frame Multicolor 3D Printer". Distinct from the K1 SE. Launch MSRP never published.
  • Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro — one name, several machines. Three hardware axes under one SKU: ribbon-cable vs Type-C printhead, 0.8 A vs 1.2 A steppers, and multiple mainboard revisions. The ribbon-cable variant is sold out; only Type-C is orderable, and most published firmware detail describes the ribbon-cable machine.
  • FlashForge AD5M — the base, non-enclosed model. The enclosed Pro is a different machine; the dimensional-accuracy figures circulating online come from the Pro.
  • Sovol SV06 ACE — distinct from the SV06, SV06 Plus and SV06 Plus ACE.

A voltage warning that applies only to the CC2. Its bed is mains AC on a solid-state relay, and Elegoo publishes two rated powers for the same auto-ranging machine: 1100 W at 220 V and 350 W at 110 V. That decomposes into roughly a 1000 W bed on 220 V and 250 W on 110 V. US buyers get roughly a quarter of the bed heating that most reviewers had. The 45–47 °C chamber figure above was measured in California, so it is a 110 V number, but bed heat-up will be substantially slower than any 220 V review suggests.

Two things worth carrying forward. The CC2 is the only machine here that got more closed over time: SSH was present up to v01.03.02.51 and was removed in v02.00.02.00 (2026-05-28), which also blocks downgrades, and its A/B rootfs discards user changes on every update. And GPL compliance is imperfect on all three of the newest machines: Creality's K2 source omits the SE machine config entirely with an open FSF complaint, Elegoo's CC2 repo is stale and unbuildable with compliance actively disputed, and QIDI ships ten compiled klippy blobs including the load-cell probe and the chamber-heater controller. Nobody has run tailscaled on any of the six.

Key differences that matter

  1. The chamber, not the nozzle, sets the material ceiling. Every machine here except the K2 SE can reach 300 °C or more, so hotend temperature is not what separates them. The QIDI Q2 is the only one with an actively heated chamber (a 280 W PTC element on an SSR, PID-controlled and clamped to 65 °C), and it is the only one where PPS-CF class materials are realistic. The CC2 comes second on a passive box measured at 45–47 °C. The other four have no chamber at all.
  1. Two machines are enclosed, four are not. The CC2 and Q2 ship in metal and glass. The Neptune and AD5M have official $39.99 enclosure kits; the K2 SE and SV06 ACE have neither.
  1. The Neptune 4 Pro has no accelerometer fitted — an unpopulated ADXL345 header, verified in firmware — so its input shaping runs on fixed values. The SV06 ACE has two LIS2DW12 sensors, one on the toolhead and one on the bed; the AD5M, CC2 and Q2 each have one; the K2 SE's is undocumented.
  1. Firmware acceleration inverts the marketing. Five machines run max_accel: 20000. The Neptune ships 5,000, a quarter of the field, despite a comparable 500 mm/s headline.
  1. Only the SV06 ACE has ever been flow-tested (24–26 mm³/s, CNC Kitchen method). Nobody has measured volumetric flow on the other five, including both $379+ machines.
  1. The CC2 owns filament handling outright. It is the only machine in the group with genuine tangle detection (a Hall sensor declared in firmware, not a renamed runout switch) and the only one with clog detection (a filament-motion encoder). It also runs two-layer runout detection where everyone else has one sensor.
  1. Multi-material is three different propositions. The CC2 Combo includes CANVAS, four colours, in the $379 price. The K2 SE supports CFS at meaningful extra cost. The QIDI Box is $228 and two independent reviewers could not make it work: Tom's found "an insurmountable amount of friction in the filament path" across two Box units plus a riser mod, and Sanladerer's four-colour print died at 39 % with metal parts in the purge bin. The other three have no multi-material path.
  1. Motion hardware is where the Q2's price goes. MGN12 linear rails on X, TMC2240 drivers on X and Y, two fully independent Z motors with Z_TILT_ADJUST run every print, 128 microsteps on Z, and a MAX6675 K-type thermocouple instead of a thermistor. It scores 90 in section C against a field average near 40.
  1. Openness splits the field three ways. The Q2 and SV06 ACE ship a real Debian userland with apt and SSH enabled. The Neptune and AD5M need a community route. The K2 SE has no documented root method at all, and the CC2 had SSH removed by a firmware update that also blocks downgrades.
  1. Power-loss recovery is claimed widely and verified once. The CC2's is hardware-sensed with an energy-reserve circuit and is the only one an independent reviewer has confirmed working. The Neptune's is documented to the interrupt line. The Q2 writes a state file. The SV06 ACE simply does not have it, and Sovol says so.
  1. Certification is not uniform. The Q2 carries MET Laboratories NRTL (valid US and Canada) plus IECEE CB. The SV06 ACE has a real FCC grant. For the Neptune, K2 SE and CC2, nothing could be verified at all.
  1. PLA is the Q2's weak case, not its strong one. With the lid closed it suffers heat creep, so the lid must be slid open. The CC2 and SV06 ACE print PLA and PETG effortlessly.
  1. Nobody has run a 12-month reliability test on any of the six, and the CC2 and K2 SE have no long-term data at all, being roughly seven months old.

Claims that don't hold up

  • The AD5M's 32 mm³/s high-flow claim is contradicted by FlashForge's own slicer profiles (PLA 25, PETG 12), and has never been independently measured.
  • The CC2's US bed is a quarter of the reviewed one. Elegoo publishes 1100 W at 220 V and 350 W at 110 V for the same auto-ranging machine. Most CC2 reviews were done on the 1000 W bed.
  • The K2 SE contradicts itself by 10× on acceleration: the store says ≤20,000 mm/s², Creality's own support page says ≤2,000.
  • The K2 SE's advertised air filtration is denied by Creality's own support page ("Air Filter: No") and by its slicer profiles, where support_air_filtration = 0.
  • The SV06 ACE camera ships a 640×480 @ 15 fps stream from 720p @ 30 fps hardware.
  • The Neptune 4 Pro's widely quoted noise and power figures (64.3 dB, 330 W peak) are measurements of the non-Pro Neptune 4. The Pro has no instrumented power measurement at all.
  • "Tangle detection", where advertised on machines other than the CC2, is a single runout switch.
  • PEI and PEEK on the Q2 are marketing. The 65 °C chamber is the binding constraint, not the 370 °C nozzle.
  • The Q2 Combo's headline feature is the part reviewers could not get working, which is why Tom's scored it 3/5 while praising the printer itself.
  • AD5M dimensional-accuracy numbers in circulation were measured on the enclosed Pro, not the base 5M.
